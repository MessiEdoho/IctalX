Find ictal events in mouse EEG, one channel at a time.

IctalX runs a trained deep learning model on a single EEG channel and surfaces candidate seizure events for you to review, confirm, and export. It assists analysis; it does not diagnose.

02

Processing pipeline

Every recording moves through the same four stages before results reach your screen.

STEP 1

Preprocessing

The selected channel is processed in blocks of 40 segments. Each block is notch-filtered at 50 Hz (zero-phase, Q = 30), its DC offset removed, then robustly normalised with the median and MAD. It is cut into 5 s segments (2,500 samples at 500 Hz) every 2.5 s. Segments with non-finite or extreme values are set aside.

→
STEP 2

Inference

A MultiScale Temporal Convolutional Network scores every 5 s segment. Three parallel dilated-convolution branches capture fine, medium, and coarse temporal scales; the fused output passes through a sigmoid to give a per-segment seizure probability in [0, 1].

→
STEP 3

Post-processing

Segment probabilities are smoothed over 3 segments and thresholded at 0.5, and consecutive positive segments are grouped into events. Events shorter than 25 s are dropped, events less than 30 s apart are merged, and events longer than 120 s are dropped.

→
STEP 4

Output

An event table with per-event descriptors, waveforms, spectrograms and confidence for human review.

Full parameters and network architecture are documented in the paper.

Run IctalX on your own machine
  1. Get the IctalX code and the model weights from the authors.
  2. Create the environment: conda env create -f environment.yml
  3. Start it: uvicorn ictalx.server:app --port 8000, with ICTALX_MODEL_DIR pointing to the weights.
  4. Open http://localhost:8000. Your recordings stay on your computer.
